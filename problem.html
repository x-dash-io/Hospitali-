Why OPDs feel this way

The patient knows what they feel. The clinician needs to know what it means.

Between those two things sits every long queue in an outpatient department.

At the patient level

Long queues discourage people with early or "minor" symptoms from seeking care promptly — which means disease progresses further before it's caught.

At the clinician level

High volumes and limited staffing mean consultation time gets spent eliciting a history, interpreting a narrative, and re-asking for clarification — before any actual assessment begins.

The actual gap

Two people, two vocabularies, one appointment slot

What the patient saysWhat the clinician needs
"Pain in the upper middle part of my stomach"Epigastric pain
"Passing loose stool three times a day"Diarrhoea, 3 episodes/day
"My head hurts in the front"Frontal headache

Every row in that table is a few minutes of consultation time spent translating, not treating. Multiply it by the length of the queue outside the door.

The conventional pathway

Where the time actually goes

01Registration
02Waiting
03History-taking
04Examination
05Investigations
06Diagnosis
07Treatment

History-taking sits in the middle of a chain that's already under pressure — and it's the one step a patient could largely do themselves, given the right interface, while they wait.

The gap, in one sentence

The patient knows what they are experiencing but may not know how to express it clinically; the clinician knows the terminology but must spend time extracting it from the narrative. Hospitali+ exists to close that gap before the consultation starts.
